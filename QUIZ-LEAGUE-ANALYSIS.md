# Quiz League — how the quiz masters write questions

Source material (2026-11-01): 9 Quiz League Facebook slides (questions 1–6, answers 3 and 5)
and photos of 3 handwritten game sheets. The Facebook page itself
(facebook.com/profile.php?id=61593313786773) can't be read without a login, so its posts
aren't included. The third handwritten sheet was photographed sideways in cursive and is
only partly readable.

This file is also the style brief for `npm run generate -- --source predicted --brief QUIZ-LEAGUE-ANALYSIS.md`.

## Game format

Each game has 5 rounds, 6 questions each (except the table round):

| # | Round | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Table Round** | One theme for the whole table, answered on paper during the game (seen: *Video games*, *Music/songs*). |
| 2 | **Hit-or-Miss** | 6 quick questions. About half have 4 options written out, the rest are open. Includes 1–2 picture/clip questions ("which film is this poster/still from?"). |
| 3 | **Theme Round** | 6 questions on one announced theme (seen: *Horror movies*, *History*). |
| 4 | **Brainteaser** | Long stories (4–6 lines) about a surprising true fact, with the key word hidden as **X** or left as "what is it?". Solvable by reasoning. |
| 5 | **Iron Тумсаг** (final) | Mixed, often with options, including "write both answers" questions (e.g. two countries with 6 Miss World titles). |

The sheet corners show scoring/difficulty codes such as `I-2-13 / II-4-12 / III-6-11` — the
round-I/II/III question picks for that game.

## How they write — patterns to expect

1. **"The only one" records.** "3 films won Palme d'Or + Best Picture, 2 won Golden Lion + Best
   Picture — which is the *only* Golden Bear + Best Picture?" They build up with numbers, then
   ask for the odd single case. Expect: only film to win the Oscar "Big Five" in X decade, only
   country to…, only person to win two Nobels in different sciences.
2. **Story first, name last.** Literature and film questions tell a backstory (debt, deadline,
   stenographer, later marriage) and only then ask for the title. The answer slide repeats the
   story with dates. Expect: *Frankenstein* (ghost-story contest), *War and Peace* (rewritten 7
   times by Sofia Tolstaya), *Gone with the Wind* (written while recovering from an injury),
   *Harry Potter* (12 rejections).
3. **"Which is NOT…" with 4 options.** Aimag borders, European cities, Nobel winners, BP drugs.
   One option is the famous trap (Gandhi, Pittsburgh). Expect: which is not a Baltic state /
   not a G7 member / not an Olympic sport / not landlocked / not a planet's moon.
4. **Classic trap answers.** Jason vs Pamela Voorhees, 20th century ends in 2000, Frankenstein
   is the doctor. Expect more of these: the Hulk's alter ego, "Beam me up, Scotty" (never said),
   Sherlock "Elementary, my dear Watson" (never in the books), Napoleon wasn't short, Big Ben is
   the bell.
5. **QI / "Today I learned" brainteasers.** Real, quirky, recent-ish (2019–2025) facts from the
   internet with a hidden X. The reasoning clue is always in the text (a cheer word → bats; a
   flooding city → gondola; a motto in a dry country → rain).
6. **Soviet & Russian culture is home turf.** Soviet comedies/dramas (Menshov, Ryazanov, Gaidai),
   Russian classics (Dostoevsky, Pushkin, Tolstoy, Bulgakov, Chekhov), fairy tales.
7. **Hollywood horror and "first film" facts.** Debut roles (Depp, Brad Pitt, Leonardo
   DiCaprio in *Critters 3*), cult horror details (The Shining's room 237, Psycho's chocolate
   syrup blood, The Exorcist).
8. **Mongolian locals, 1–2 per game.** An actor/director's career, an aimag fact, a rare word
   picked from 4 look-alikes (лавс/нэвс/цавс/салс), the 12-animal hours, a UB landmark's past.
9. **Brands & celebrities.** K-pop group membership, brand ambassadors (Dua Lipa → YSL), Netflix
   reality shows, record holders (Miss World, Eurovision, Ballon d'Or).
10. **Picture and clip questions.** Posters, stills, rice/food photos, trailers. The app can't
    show those, so the predicted questions turn them into text clues.
